The problem

Every measure you hold arrives after the point where you could have acted on it.

At the front, the attendance register: applications, attendees, businesses supported. Counted at the door. It tells you the room was full.

At the back, the accounts: revenue, headcount, survival. Real, and far too late.

Between them, nothing measures whether a company can actually sell what it has built, what is wrong with it, or whether it got better. That gap is the only place anyone can still act.

Why the method is public

Nobody adopts a measure they are not allowed to inspect.

Every standard worth the name is published: accounting standards, cyber certification schemes, clinical instruments, impact frameworks. The ones that stayed secret stayed products.

  • You can read every question and scale before anyone answers one.
  • You can reproduce the arithmetic by hand from the published rules.
  • You can verify determinism yourself against our public test vectors.
  • You can check any deployment against the version it claims to run.
  • What stays ours is the diagnostic library, the routing, and the register of who has been measured.

Specification v1.4

The method

This page is the specification, not a description of one. Every version stays published at its own permanent address, unedited, so a score taken today can still be audited in five years against the exact rules that produced it.

Permanent address: /method/v1.4/ · Superseded versions remain live indefinitely

1. What is measured

Thirty seven questions. Thirty two measure capability, what the company has built. Five measure momentum, whether the engine is producing output now. The two are scored separately and averaged, because the gap between them is itself diagnostic.

Capability questions sit in a four by four matrix: four go-to-market stages against four root causes, two questions per cell.

StageThe job it does
DemandCreate enough qualified attention from the right kind of buyer
Sales ProcessTurn attention into qualified opportunities that progress
ClosingConvert late-stage opportunities into signed business with predictable timing
ExpandTurn signed customers into renewals, adoption and expansion
Root causeThe question it asks
ProductIs the proposition, value and business case sharp enough?
PeopleAre the right people, with the right judgement and accountability, owning it?
ProcessIs there a defined, evidenced way of working that can be inspected?
RepeatabilityIs what works captured, codified and reused rather than reinvented?

2. The scales

Both scales store a value from one to five. They ask different things, so the labels differ. Capability is scored on evidence and consistency, not intent.

Capability scale · questions 1 to 32

ValueLabelMeaning
1Not in placeThis is not true in practice yet, or we do not really do it.
2InconsistentIt happens in some cases, but not reliably, consistently or with clear evidence.
3Partly in placeWe have a working version, but usage, ownership or evidence is uneven.
4ConsistentIt is part of how we normally operate and we can evidence it.
5Proven and improvingIt works, is evidenced, and we review or improve it over time.

Momentum scale · questions 33 to 37

ValueLabelMeaning
1Well below targetThis area is materially short of where it needs to be.
2Below targetThere is some movement, but it is not enough or not reliable.
3Partly on trackThere is usable movement, but it is uneven or not yet enough.
4On trackThis area is broadly where it needs to be.
5StrongThis area is ahead of target or clearly working.

3. The arithmetic

Reproducible by hand. Nothing is weighted by sector, company size or anything else, and no generative model touches the scoring path. The same answers return the same score today and in five years.

// every step, in order
cell        = mean(its 2 questions)                  // 1 to 5
stage       = mean(the 4 cells in that row)          // 1 to 5
rootCause   = mean(that cause across all 4 stages)   // 1 to 5
capability  = mean(all 16 cells) / 5 * 100          // 0 to 100
momentum    = mean(the 5 momentum answers) / 5 * 100
score       = round((capability + momentum) / 2)

Bands

BandScoreWhat it means
Base Camp0 to 39Foundations still forming. Pick one stage and install the repeatable basics.
Ascent40 to 59Moving, but what is built and what is done daily are not pulling together.
Ridge60 to 79A working motion. The next move is repeatability at scale.
Summit80 to 100Repeatable and defensible. Focus shifts to expansion and durability.

A 58 and a 61 are functionally the same engine. A few points either side of a band boundary is noise, and the method says so rather than letting the band imply precision it does not have.

4. The diagnosis

The score is the headline. The diagnosis is the point. A deterministic rule engine identifies three things, in this order:

  • The primary stage. Where the symptom is loudest.
  • The local root cause. Within that stage, the weakest of the four causes.
  • The systemic root cause. Across all four stages, the weakest cause overall.

When the systemic cause is materially weaker than the local one, the report says so explicitly: this is not just a Closing problem, Process is weak across your whole engine. That is usually the bigger prize, because fixing it lifts more than one stage.

Every diagnosis rolls up into one of four plain-language lenses.

LensWhat it means
VolumeNot enough qualified demand is being created. Symptom: thin pipeline.
EfficiencyOpportunities exist but do not convert consistently. Symptom: low win rate.
ControlThe forecast cannot be trusted. Symptom: late-stage slippage, deals moving on seller opinion.
ExpansionCustomers are not renewing or expanding at the rate needed.

What is not published here. The rule precedence and thresholds that select the lens, and the sixteen-cell library that turns a stage and cause into a written diagnosis and a set of actions. Those are the product. The measurement is the standard; the prescription is not. Licensees receive both in full before signing, under the licence.

5. Capability against momentum

Most assessments collapse these into one number and lose the finding. The gap between them is a diagnosis in itself.

CapabilityMomentumWhat it usually means
LowLowThe engine is not built and not producing. Pick one stage and install the basics.
LowHighResults are coming from founder effort. Capacity-limited: when the hustle stops, so does the output.
HighLowThe engine is built and nobody is running it. Usually inspection rhythm, ownership or cadence.
HighHighBuilt and producing. Focus shifts to expansion and durability.

The read

Your main limitation is Control. It shows up most strongly in Closing, where the local weakness is Process. Process is also the weakest root cause across your whole engine, so this is systemic, not a Closing problem. The measure most under pressure is forecast accuracy.

In plain English

Deals reach late stage but the close date cannot be trusted. Pricing, approval, procurement and legal steps are being discovered too late, so deals slip out of the quarter. Because the same weakness sits in Sales Process too, it will keep repeating: every new cohort of deals hits the same wall.

What not to do

Do not push for verbal commitment, do not discount to close, and do not add more pipeline pressure. Each of those hides the real problem and buys a quarter at the cost of a year.

The first three moves

  • This week. For your top five live deals, write down the next approval step, its owner and its date, and confirm all three with the buyer.
  • Next 30 days. Add those fields to the CRM and make them the spine of the weekly deal review.
  • 90 day proof. Stage age in late stages, slipped close dates, forecast variance. Then take the measure again.

How that score was produced

Every number above is reproducible from the published rules. Nothing is hidden between the answers and the result.

// cell averages from the 32 capability answers
                Product  People  Process  Repeat.
Demand            3.5     3.0      2.5      2.5   // stage 2.9
Sales Process     3.5     3.0      2.0      2.0   // stage 2.6
Closing           3.0     2.5      1.5      2.0   // stage 2.3  <- weakest
Expand            2.0     2.0      1.5      1.5   // stage 1.8

// root cause columns
Product 3.0   People 2.6   Process 1.9  <- weakest overall   Repeatability 2.0

capability = mean(16 cells) = 2.4  ->  (2.4 / 5) * 100 = 48
momentum   = mean(5 answers) = 2.8  ->  (2.8 / 5) * 100 = 56
score      = round((48 + 56) / 2) = 52   ->  band: Ascent

Expand scores lowest of the four stages, but the engine does not send this company to fix Expand. New business is not yet working, so an expansion finding is suppressed and the diagnosis routes to the stage that matters now. That precedence is what stops a measure producing technically correct, commercially useless advice.

When a peer benchmark gets published

Not until there is a defensible cohort: provisionally at least 100 comparable observations per segment, with the exact threshold set against the observed distribution on statistical advice. At 31 companies measured in total, no segment is close, so no percentile is shown anywhere in the product. We could display one today and it would be fiction.

For institutions

The method is free. The reporting is what you license.

Companies are never charged. Institutions license the cohort view, the routing and the reporting: what moved, for whom, after which intervention, on one instrument that makes two programmes comparable for the first time.

Banks

Two buyers, one instrument

Public bodies

The quarterly report as a by-product

Baseline at intake, endline at exit, per company, across every delivery partner. The sentence changes from "we put 200 companies through" to "we measured 200, this proportion moved, after this intervention". Either the authority licenses it centrally or each provider funds it from money already allocated.

Universities and accelerators

Evidence attached to the next bid

Intake and exit positions for every team, the distribution of root causes across the cohort, and movement per team. Comparable across cohorts and across years, because the instrument does not change.

Investors and funds

Portfolio movement from day one

Your criteria, weighted by you, applied consistently to inbound. Then the same instrument across the portfolio twice a year, so the evidence pack for the next fund exists before you need it. It cannot be reconstructed later.

First engagement is always the same shape: one cohort, one quarter, baseline and endline, one report. If the report is not something you would put in front of a funder, you do not continue.

Limits

What this does not do.

Published here rather than conceded in a meeting. A measure that will not state its own limits should not be trusted with anyone's decisions.

  • It does not predict growth. It measures the commercial conditions that have to be true before a company can grow, and whether they are improving. That is upstream of growth, not a forecast of it.
  • It does not predict or improve the odds of funding. No claim connecting a score to a funding outcome will be made until outcome evidence supports one, and there is none yet.
  • It is not a credit or risk signal. It must not be used as an input to lending, affordability, pricing or eligibility decisions.
  • The data is self-reported. It is what the company says about how it sells, stated on every report. Answering generously produces a flattering number and no movement at the rescore, which is the only part anyone cares about.
  • It does not rank companies against each other. A score is a position against a fixed reference, not a league table.
  • It does not claim causation. A company that moved after an intervention moved after an intervention. The report says that and no more.
  • There is no peer benchmark. The sample is not large enough for one to be fair. See the threshold in the version history.
  • Nothing unverified is dressed as fact. Every figure on this site that is not confirmed appears as a marked placeholder.

About
